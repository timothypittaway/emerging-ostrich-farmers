# Research design

## Overall approach

Exploratory, because no earlier research on this question existed to build on. The study used triangulation, combining quantitative and qualitative methods, within a mainly interpretive framework. Qualitative analysis drew on grounded theory and content analysis.

## Stages

1. **Literature and content analysis.** A review of research and industry sources, including text-mining of published ostrich research to map research themes over time.
2. **Pilot studies** to test the approach and instruments.
3. **Situational analysis of the industry.** Micro environment (products, geographic competition, competitive forces) and macro environment (political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, environmental and legal influences), plus interviews with industry stakeholders.
4. **Multiple case studies** of five emerging ostrich farmer programmes in the Eastern Cape: Peddie, Rockhurst, Hlumani Co-operative, Zamukwanda and Mimosadale.
5. **Questionnaire** with farmers (see `questionnaire-structure.csv`), designed from the literature on what determines smallholder market participation.
6. **Focus groups** that mixed farmers with mentors, extension officers, programme managers and Department of Agriculture staff, to test and deepen questionnaire themes.
7. **Participation capacity instrument** developed from observation, questionnaire and focus group data (see `../instrument/`).

## Qualitative analysis

Notes from interviews and focus groups were written up into primary documents once saturation was reached. These were coded in ATLAS.ti, then the codes were cleaned, grouped into categories and refined in repeated passes for consistency. Code frequencies were tabulated, and network views were used to show relationships between themes.

## Ethics

- Approval from the NMMU Research Ethics Committee
- Voluntary participation, with the right to withdraw, explained in English and isiXhosa
- Informed consent after the aims and the participant's role were explained
- Permission from farmers, mentors and stakeholders before fieldwork
- Anonymity through reference codes instead of names

## Limitations

- The researcher's prior involvement in the industry helped with access but risked assumptions, so a detailed structured question set was used.
- Samples were small. Most Eastern Cape programmes were included, but some were inactive or farmers were unavailable.
- Published information on the supply chain was scarce, so popular and corporate sources and preliminary stakeholder surveys were used.
- Some participants needed isiXhosa translation. A translator attended all fieldwork, with terms agreed in advance and multilingual checks.
- The capacity instrument is a prototype and has not been validated.
